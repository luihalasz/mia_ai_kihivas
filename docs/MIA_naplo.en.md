## 1. Summary

The goal was to present the concept of a new burst-pouch filling–clipping–sealing machine (MIA, i.e. Manyika 2) well enough for management to give it the green light. I brought the engineering knowledge: dimensions, the process and the problems of Manyika 1. Together with the AI we turned that into:

- the **parametric 3D model** (Blender + Python, `blender/mia_model_v2.py`),
- the **3D configurator in the browser** with the cycle animation (Three.js, `configurator.html`),
- the **renders** (Blender Cycles),
- the **website** in three languages (HU/EN/IT),
- a **~1-minute promo video**,
- and this **log**.

Almost nothing was drawn by hand: everything is generated by code, so every dimension is a parameter that lives in one place.

## 3. How it went

### Evening 1 – Claude Chat: describing the machine and the model

First I described the machine in words: the pouch sizes (PE 550×180 / 800×180, alu 285×220 / 450×220), where the clip and the seal go, the fill heights for each fill size, the two fill cylinders (ISO and water glass), the vacuum cups, the smoothing jaws and the housing. From that, Claude wrote a **parametric Blender Python script** that builds the whole machine and renders images of it.

We refined it over many rounds. I pointed out what did not match reality, and the AI fixed it. A few examples:
- the fill tube must never dip into the material, and when retracted it must stay 25 mm above the film;
- the vacuum must not pull the side seams apart, only open the mouth of the pouch;
- the machine height must be sized for the large pouch for both presets.

**Where we got stuck:**
- **Blender in the cloud:** Blender could not be downloaded into Chat's cloud sandbox, so it rendered with the `bpy` Python package on the CPU only. That was slow: one image took minutes.
- **Uploading:** pushing to GitHub from Chat's sandbox was not possible. So I downloaded the files and uploaded them by hand on the GitHub website ("Add file → Upload files"). Once I forgot to press "Commit changes" at the end.

### Evening 2 – Claude Chat: the controller and the configurator basics

Next to the model we needed something anyone can try without installing anything. Claude wrote a **single-file Three.js configurator**:
- fill-size picker with all 8 sizes (330 ml – 2.25 l);
- animation of the full 17-step cycle: vacuum, filling, smoothing, clipping, sealing, release;
- speed control and a Start button;
- a warning if a size would collide with the fill tube.

This is also where we fixed the control logic: the two-hand start with two green buttons, and the order and timing of the steps.

**Where we got stuck:**
- Transparent films flickered over each other (z-fighting). We solved it with a fixed draw order and `depthWrite:false`.

### Day 3 – Claude Code: the same, only much faster

This is when I found out that I get much better results in **code mode** (Claude Code), because the AI works directly on my own computer:
- it installed Git itself and cloned the repository;
- it rendered with Blender on my own graphics card, in seconds instead of minutes;
- it checked its own work right away in the built-in browser: took screenshots and checked the phone layout too;
- I just said what I wanted, and showed it with a screenshot or a sketch when something was off.

What got done that day:
1. **Website**:
   - black menu bar on every page with its own Equipment R&D logo;
   - Products menu (Manyika 2 · MIA, Manyika), Accessories, Contacts, Career, STORE;
   - on the home page a black–metal–white gradient hero section with the rendered machine.
2. **Three languages**: HU/EN/IT switch in the top-right corner. The choice is kept from page to page and is passed on to the configurator.
3. **Configurator rework**:
   - clean interface: only the fill-size picker, the automatic animation and the rotatable model are left;
   - shaded look with outlines instead of the sketchy one;
   - the tablet moved onto an arm, because it used to stick into the machine wall;
   - the camera orbits around the middle of the film as it sits in the machine.
4. **New renders** on my own GPU: the drawer pulled out with a pouch; the machine on its stand with the full three-colour stack light and a black screen.
5. **Clip pair drawing** based on my hand sketch. It took two corrections until the end of the C profile lined up exactly with the waist of the figure-8.
6. **~1-minute video**, made entirely in Blender, from code. The first version was 41 s long, 720p, with Hungarian captions. After my feedback it became:
   - 61 s, 5 shots, 1400 frames, English captions, better compression and more render samples. 1080p would have taken ~4.5 hours of rendering, so because of the deadline it stayed 720p (~2 hours);
   - whatever is being talked about gets a yellow outline that flashes twice (made in the compositor from a separate "mask" render layer);
   - in the four-machine shot every drawer is open, and the operator closes them one by one from left to right;
   - a Polinvent logo and "Coming soon in 2027" at the end;
   - a "hype" trailer score synthesised in code (numpy), with a sound effect on every flash.

   The video shows these advantages over Manyika 1:
   - pressure filling without splashing instead of direct peristaltic filling;
   - closed housing: the cylinder can't pinch the operator's hand;
   - loading the film with a drawer instead of threading it blind;
   - two-hand start with two green buttons;
   - two adjustable pouch sizes;
   - smoothing jaws: the corners don't fold in;
   - vacuum cups instead of clamping with a cylinder, so the film doesn't slip;
   - 4 MIAs fit in the space of one Manyika 1, and they need 1 operator instead of 5.
7. **Downloads**: the 3D model (.glb, .blend, STEP), the model's Python code, the GitHub link and this log, on the MIA and Contacts pages. The log can be read in all three languages.

**Where we got stuck:**
- **Git was missing** from the computer: Claude installed it (winget), I approved it.
- **Push permission:** only I can sign in to GitHub. Claude prepared and committed everything, and I ran `git push` in the terminal with a browser sign-in. I also asked it not to commit until I had reviewed the changes, and it kept to that.
- **Windows PowerShell quirks:** scripts with accented characters broke, and because variable names are case-insensitive an image was overwritten with a blank one. Claude noticed and fixed it.
- **Blender 5.2 API change:** the setting for video export was renamed (`media_type = 'VIDEO'`). Fixed with one line, based on the error message.
- **Image framing:** at first the top of the stack light was cut off on the hero image, and the screen showed an odd blue rectangle. A camera adjustment and a "fully black, cool" screen fixed it.
- **Yellow outline:** in the mask layer the other highlight parts (e.g. the glass housing) hid the film like a black wall, so its outline did not show. Those parts are now transparent until it is their turn.
- **Copying the four machines:** I had already moved the original machine before copying it, so the copies ended up in the wrong place. It showed on the test frame and was solved by changing the order.
- **Wording:** I did not know the word "Skid", so it became "Ellátóegység" (supply unit).

## 5. Lessons learned

- **Chat is good for thinking, Code is good for doing.** The concept and the logic of the model worked well in Chat. As soon as files, renders, git and a browser on my own computer were involved, code mode was often faster.
- **A picture says more.** A screenshot or a hand sketch with a red line got the point across faster than any description.
- **The parametric model pays off.** The configurator, the renders and the video are all made from the same code, so a change of dimension shows up everywhere at once.
- **I stay in control.** Commits and pushes only happened with my approval, and I did the signing in myself.
